Privacy Policy

Draft reviewed October 17, 2026 · The effective date will be set after approval and publication.

Important: this page is still a draft and must not be published yet. QuitTrack is operated by Diego Ferreira, an individual; the privacy contact is quittrack.support@proton.me. The legal bases below are working hypotheses, not a conclusion. They still require legal review and confirmation of the actual Google AdMob/UMP settings, international transfers, and Google Play disclosures.

1. Who is responsible

QuitTrack is an Android app for tracking progress in stopping unwanted habits. The controller responsible for app data is Diego Ferreira, an individual (the “controller”). For privacy questions or requests, write to quittrack.support@proton.me.

2. Information you enter and what the app stores on your device

QuitTrack does not require an account and no QuitTrack-operated server was identified in the audited code. The app stores locally, in Android's private app storage, the content you create and settings needed to operate. This may include:

  • habit names, categories, start dates, colors, and archive periods;
  • consumption amounts and frequencies used to calculate saved money and recovered time, plus the associated currency code and time zone;
  • relapse dates and notes;
  • personal reasons and journal entries;
  • targets, rewards, and progress milestones;
  • language, theme, and notification preferences; and
  • security preferences and operational PIN-lock information, such as failed attempts and their times.

The text you choose to enter may reveal intimate or health-related information, including addiction, consumption, wellbeing, or personal life. Avoid entering details you do not want stored on the device or included in an exported file.

3. Purposes

The app uses local data to provide features you request: recording habits and history, calculating metrics, showing targets and statistics, saving preferences, scheduling selected notifications, and protecting access when you enable a PIN or biometrics. Providing a requested feature is a basis to assess for each strictly necessary item of data—LGPD Article 7(V) and GDPR Article 6(1)(b) only where a contractual relationship and demonstrable necessity exist. The controller must still confirm the service terms, necessity of each item, and territorial application of each law; local storage alone does not establish a legal basis. Android's technical permission is not, by itself, a legal basis either.

Reasons, journal entries, and notes are free-text fields and may contain sensitive personal data, including health data. The audit did not establish an applicable condition under LGPD Article 11 or GDPR Article 9 for such content. Accordingly, this policy does not claim that processing any sensitive data already has a valid basis. Before retaining this use, the controller must decide and validate a specific legal condition or adopt a product measure to avoid/restrict entry and processing of this content. Local operation reduces sharing by the app but does not remove obligations that may apply.

For ads and data processed by Google Mobile Ads, the bases and roles depend on the purposes and terms applicable to Google itself, which this audit did not determine. Choices shown by Google UMP control the configured privacy flow but are not automatically the legal basis for all Google processing, nor a basis QuitTrack can attribute to the provider. The controller must separately confirm personalization, delivery, security/fraud prevention, diagnostics, and measurement, including whether any processing requires consent. Showing or not showing a UMP form alone does not prove valid consent.

UMP checks and retains choices to operate the privacy flow; the purpose, role of each party, applicable basis, and retention for that information must be checked against the configuration and Google's terms. No routine processing based on a legal obligation was identified in the app. That basis will be considered only if a concrete obligation applies, with data and retention limited to what is necessary. Export and backup are initiated by you and create local files; the requested operation may be assessed as providing that feature within the limits above. If you choose to share or store the file with another service, that recipient processes its copy under its own terms, and your choice of destination does not by itself determine that third party's legal basis.

These hypotheses are not legal advice or a compliance determination. Before publication, the controller must document the basis for each purpose, data necessity, and especially the applicable condition for any sensitive data. References: Brazilian LGPD and the GDPR.

4. Local storage and security

QuitTrack content is kept in the app's private storage on your device. MMKV storage is configured with local encryption, and the PIN credential is kept using Android SecureStore. Biometric authentication is requested from the operating system; QuitTrack does not receive or store your fingerprint, face, or biometric template.

These safeguards do not guarantee absolute security. The key used to protect MMKV storage is included in the app, and the PIN is not a recoverable account password. The app has no PIN recovery and no account/server for recovering data. Protect your device and keep its operating system up to date.

5. Ads and Google Mobile Ads

When ads are enabled in a build, QuitTrack uses the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob) to show banners in specific areas to Free users. Premium users do not receive these banners. Ad availability and personalization depend on SDK configuration, privacy choices, and applicable rules.

According to Google's Android Google Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure, the SDK may automatically collect and share IP address (including to estimate a device's general location), product interactions, diagnostic information, and device/account identifiers such as Android Advertising ID, for advertising, service analytics, and fraud prevention. The SDK may also use Android advertising permissions and APIs. QuitTrack requests non-personalized ads, but this does not mean that the SDK does not process technical data or identifiers. QuitTrack does not include habit names, relapse notes, or journal text as banner targeting parameters; we could not audit every transmission made internally by the SDK.

Google handles this data under its own terms and privacy policy. Processing may take place in countries other than yours. See Google's Privacy Policy, How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services, and the Android Google Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure.

6. Google UMP consent and privacy choices

QuitTrack integrates the Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) to refresh consent information, show a message when Google's configuration requires one, and provide privacy options when UMP says that an entry point is required. Consent state is managed by the SDK. The app allows ads to initialize/load only when the SDK state allows ad requests; if an update fails, the SDK's previous state may be used, and if that state cannot be read, ads remain disabled.

The message shown depends on the settings, regions, and messages published in the AdMob account. The app owner must review those external settings. UMP communicates with Google services to update status and display messages; technical request details and consent choices are handled under Google's documentation and privacy policy. The app code does not reveal every network datum processed by UMP.

When UMP requires a privacy-options entry point, it appears in Settings. Use that in-app option to review choices through this flow. AdMob message settings and the availability of this flow must be confirmed before distribution. See UMP documentation for Android and Google's Privacy & messaging guidance.

7. Notifications and permissions

QuitTrack notifications are scheduled locally on the device for achievements, targets, and, if enabled with a time you choose, a daily quote. They are not sent by a QuitTrack server. You can disable them in the app or Android settings. Android may require notification permission. If you choose detailed notification content, a notification may show the habit name and milestone; the generic mode avoids those details. What appears on the lock screen also depends on device settings.

The app uses Internet for ad/consent integrations and related libraries; notification, biometric, and vibration permissions are used for their corresponding features. We found no direct request for location, contacts, camera, or microphone. The app uses device language, time zone, and theme for the experience without requesting GPS coordinates.

8. Backup, export, and sharing

You can start a readable export or, with Premium, create a restorable backup and select a backup file. Before sharing, the app warns that these files contain personal information and are not encrypted. The JSON file may contain habits, relapses, reasons, journal entries, targets, rewards, achievements, and preferences, depending on the file type. Export is initiated by you and uses Android's share sheet; the selected destination—for example, another app or storage service—then handles the file under its own practices. QuitTrack cannot control copies sent to or retained by third parties.

Temporary files are written to app cache and the app attempts to remove them after the operation. A system error may prevent immediate cleanup. Android automatic backup is disabled in the app configuration. Keep shared files in a safe place and delete copies you no longer want to retain.

9. Diagnostics, analytics, and third-party services

Our audit of the current code and dependencies found no product analytics service, QuitTrack-operated backend, or dedicated crash-reporting SDK. However, Google Mobile Ads SDK may process diagnostics and interactions as described above. Android, Google Play Services, UMP, and any app selected for sharing may carry out their own processing. The presence of a permission or library alone does not establish every datum actually transmitted by those components.

10. Retention and deletion

QuitTrack data remains on your device until you delete individual records through an available in-app feature, clear the app's storage using Android controls, or uninstall it. Deleting a habit in the app also deletes records linked to that habit. There is no QuitTrack account to delete and no in-app feature to erase all local data at once or remotely erase data held by third parties. Exported copies, shared files, and data processed by Google or other apps must be managed with those services.

Temporary cache files are removed after an operation when the system allows. Retention of data processed by Google is governed by Google's policies and controls.

11. Your rights and requests

Depending on applicable law, you may request information about processing and exercise rights such as access, correction, deletion, objection, restriction, and portability where provided and applicable. You may also withdraw consent where it is the basis used, without affecting the lawfulness of earlier processing, and complain to a competent supervisory authority. For data stored only on your device, use available app features or Android storage controls; to contact the controller, write to quittrack.support@proton.me. Do not send your PIN or journal contents by email. For data processed directly by Google, use Google's controls and channels.

12. Children and intended audience

QuitTrack is intended exclusively for people aged 18 or older. People under 18 are not part of the audience for which the app is intended. The app does not implement an internal age check; this statement describes the intended audience and is not a technical access restriction. Google Play's audience and age-rating settings and advertising options must reflect this decision and be verified before distribution.

13. International transfers

The app's primary content storage is local. Use of AdMob and UMP may send technical and consent data to Google services, which may process it outside your country. The app audit did not identify every destination country, receiving entity, duration, responsibility, or mechanism applicable to each flow. Before publication and international distribution, the controller must confirm these elements in Google's current terms and settings and document the applicable legal basis and valid mechanism for each transfer. For transfers subject to the LGPD, also consult the ANPD International Data Transfer Regulation. This policy does not claim universal compliance with every country's laws.

14. Changes to this policy

This policy may change when the app, SDKs, settings, or applicable requirements change. The date at the top shows the latest revision. Significant changes should be communicated through appropriate means and reflected in Google Play information.
